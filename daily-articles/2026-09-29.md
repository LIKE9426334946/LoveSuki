# Why Popcorn Pops

A popcorn kernel looks small, dry, and solid. Yet after a short time in a hot pan or microwave, it can suddenly turn inside out and become many times larger. This dramatic change is possible because each kernel is a tiny pressure container with exactly the right structure.

Popcorn is a special type of corn. Each kernel has a hard outer shell called the hull. Inside are starch, protein, and a small amount of water. The water is important even though the kernel appears completely dry. A typical kernel contains enough moisture to produce steam when it is heated.

At first, heat moves through the hull and warms the material inside. The water begins to change into water vapor. Because the tough hull does not let the vapor escape easily, pressure builds inside the kernel. At the same time, the hot water and pressure soften the hard starch into a thick, flexible material.

Eventually, the hull can no longer hold the increasing pressure. It breaks open very quickly. The sudden drop in pressure allows the water vapor to expand, pushing the softened starch outward. The starch forms many tiny bubbles, much like foam. As it meets the cooler air, it rapidly cools and becomes solid. The white shape that we eat is therefore the expanded material that was once packed inside the kernel.

Most types of corn do not pop as successfully. Their outer layers may allow steam to escape, or their internal structure may not create the right pressure. Popcorn varieties have been selected because their strong hulls and suitable moisture levels make popping more reliable.

Even in a good bag of popcorn, a few kernels may remain unpopped. Some have small cracks in their hulls, so vapor escapes before enough pressure develops. Others may contain too little or too much water. Kernels can slowly lose moisture during storage, especially when a container is left open. Keeping unpopped popcorn in a tightly closed container helps protect the moisture balance.

Oil is useful but not essential for popping. In a pan, oil transfers heat evenly from the hot surface to the kernels and can add flavor. Hot air poppers use moving heated air instead. In a microwave, electromagnetic energy causes certain molecules, including water molecules, to move and produce heat. All three methods have the same final goal: heating the water inside each kernel quickly and evenly.

Timing matters because popped corn can burn while the remaining kernels are still heating. When the sounds slow to several seconds between pops, most people stop the heat. Opening a covered pan or microwave bag also requires care because hot steam can escape.

Plain popcorn is a whole-grain food and can be a simple snack. However, butter, sugar, caramel, and large amounts of salt can greatly change its nutritional content. The basic puff itself is created by a neat combination of moisture, heat, pressure, and starch.

The next time you hear popcorn exploding in a pan, imagine the process inside each kernel. Water becomes vapor, pressure rises, the hull breaks, and soft starch expands into a light, crunchy structure—all in a fraction of a second.

## Important Vocabulary

1. **kernel** — the seed or inner edible part of a grain; 谷粒，玉米粒
2. **hull** — the hard outer covering of a seed or grain; 外壳
3. **starch** — a substance in foods such as corn, rice, and potatoes that provides energy; 淀粉
4. **moisture** — a small amount of water contained in something; 水分
5. **vapor** — a substance in its gas form, especially one normally found as a liquid; 蒸气
6. **pressure** — force produced when something pushes against a surface; 压力
7. **expand** — to become larger or spread out; 膨胀，扩大
8. **foam** — a mass of small bubbles formed in or on a liquid or soft material; 泡沫
9. **transfer** — to move energy or something else from one place to another; 传递，转移
10. **nutritional** — related to food and its effect on health and growth; 营养的

## Useful Grammar and Sentence Patterns

1. **“At the same time, + clause”**  
   Use this pattern to describe two processes happening together.  
   Example: “At the same time, the hot water and pressure soften the hard starch.”

2. **“can no longer + verb”**  
   Use this pattern when someone or something has lost the ability to continue an action.  
   Example: “Eventually, the hull can no longer hold the increasing pressure.”

3. **“especially when + clause”**  
   Use this pattern to emphasize a condition in which something is particularly true.  
   Example: “Kernels can slowly lose moisture during storage, especially when a container is left open.”
