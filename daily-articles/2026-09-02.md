# Why Food Tastes Different When You Cannot Smell

Have you ever noticed that food seems less enjoyable when you have a blocked nose? A favorite soup may taste plain, and different candies may seem almost the same. This happens because what we commonly call “taste” is actually a mixture of several senses. The tongue provides some information, but the nose adds much of the detail that makes food interesting.

The tongue can recognize five basic tastes: sweet, sour, salty, bitter, and umami. Umami is the rich, savory taste found in foods such as mushrooms, meat, and cheese. These basic tastes are important because they help us judge what we eat. Sweetness can signal energy, salt is needed by the body, and bitterness may warn us about harmful substances. However, the tongue alone cannot easily tell the difference between an apple and a pear.

Most of that difference comes from smell. When you chew food, tiny chemical molecules travel from the back of your mouth into your nose. There, special smell receptors detect them and send signals to the brain. This process is called retronasal smelling. It is different from smelling food through your nostrils before you take a bite, although both processes use the same sensory system.

The brain combines smell, taste, temperature, and texture into one complete experience called flavor. Sound and appearance can influence it too. A crisp apple creates a particular sound when you bite it, while its color may lead you to expect sweetness or freshness. The brain usually joins all these signals so smoothly that you do not notice them as separate pieces of information.

When your nose is blocked by a cold, fewer food molecules reach the smell receptors. Your tongue still detects sugar or salt, but many specific details disappear. That is why you may know that a candy is sweet without being able to identify its fruit flavor. You can test this safely by holding your nose, eating a small piece of familiar food, and then releasing your nose while you continue chewing. The flavor will often appear much stronger at that moment.

Understanding flavor can also help you enjoy food more carefully. Try slowing down and noticing its smell before eating. Pay attention to texture, temperature, and the way the flavor changes as you chew. If food seems tasteless during a cold, strong textures, warm temperatures, or a little extra sourness may make a meal more satisfying. Still, avoid adding too much salt simply because the food seems weak. Your tongue can detect the salt even when your nose cannot provide the full flavor. Eating is not the work of one sense; it is a small performance created by the mouth, nose, and brain together.

## Important Vocabulary

1. **blocked** — closed or prevented from allowing something to pass through; 堵塞的
2. **mixture** — a combination of different things; 混合物，混合
3. **savory** — having a salty or rich taste rather than a sweet one; 咸香的，鲜美的
4. **substance** — a particular type of solid, liquid, or gas; 物质
5. **molecule** — the smallest unit of a substance that keeps its chemical properties; 分子
6. **receptor** — a cell or structure that receives a signal or stimulus; 感受器
7. **sensory** — related to the physical senses; 感官的
8. **texture** — the way food or another material feels; 质地，口感
9. **identify** — to recognize and name something; 识别，确认
10. **satisfying** — making you feel pleased or giving you what you need; 令人满足的

## Useful Grammar and Sentence Patterns

1. **“what we commonly call … is actually …”**  
   Use this pattern to correct or improve a common understanding.  
   Example: “What we commonly call ‘taste’ is actually a mixture of several senses.”

2. **“That is why + result”**  
   Use this pattern to introduce the result of the explanation given before it.  
   Example: “That is why you may know that a candy is sweet without being able to identify its fruit flavor.”

3. **“while + contrasting clause”**  
   Use **while** to compare two different facts or situations.  
   Example: “A crisp apple creates a particular sound when you bite it, while its color may lead you to expect sweetness.”
