# How Your Body Clock Keeps Time

You may wake up a few minutes before your alarm or feel sleepy at almost the same time every night. These experiences are not simply habits. Your body contains an internal timing system, often called the body clock, that organizes many activities across a cycle of about twenty-four hours.

This daily pattern is known as a circadian rhythm. It influences sleep, body temperature, digestion, attention, and the release of hormones. Different organs have their own rhythms, but a small area in the brain acts as the main clock. It is located above the place where nerves from the eyes cross. This position allows it to receive useful information about light.

Light is the strongest signal for setting the body clock. Special cells in the eyes detect brightness, even when you are not carefully looking at anything. In the morning, light tells the brain that a new day has begun. The main clock then helps the body become more alert and coordinates the timing of other organs.

As evening arrives and the environment becomes darker, the brain begins to increase production of melatonin. This hormone does not immediately make you fall asleep, but it tells the body that nighttime is approaching. Bright light late in the evening, especially from lamps and screens held close to the face, can delay this signal. The effect depends on the brightness, color, duration, and timing of the light.

The body clock is not exactly twenty-four hours in every person, so it needs regular adjustment. Morning light usually moves the clock earlier, while strong light late at night can move it later. Meals, exercise, social activity, and temperature also provide timing clues, although light generally has the greatest influence.

Problems appear when daily behavior strongly conflicts with the internal clock. Jet lag occurs when air travel moves a person rapidly across several time zones. The clock still follows the old schedule and needs time to adjust. Night-shift workers face a similar difficulty because they must stay alert when the body expects darkness and sleep when the outside world is bright.

You can support a stable rhythm with a few simple choices. Try to wake up at a similar time each day and get outdoor light during the morning. Keep evening light softer, and avoid very bright screens just before bed. Regular exercise and meal times can also strengthen daily signals. A cool, dark bedroom helps the body follow its nighttime program.

However, the body clock cannot replace the need for enough sleep. Following a regular schedule may make sleep easier, but sleeping too little still reduces attention, learning, and mood. The goal is to give your internal clock clear signals and enough time to complete the rest your brain and body require.

## Important Vocabulary

1. **internal** — existing or happening inside the body or an object; 内部的
2. **circadian** — relating to a natural cycle of about twenty-four hours; 昼夜节律的
3. **rhythm** — a regular pattern of movement, activity, or change; 节律，规律
4. **hormone** — a chemical substance that carries messages within the body; 激素
5. **coordinate** — to organize different parts so that they work together; 协调
6. **alert** — awake and able to notice or think clearly; 清醒的，警觉的
7. **melatonin** — a hormone that helps signal nighttime to the body; 褪黑素
8. **duration** — the amount of time that something continues; 持续时间
9. **adjustment** — a small change made to improve or correct something; 调整
10. **conflict** — to be different from or opposed to something; 冲突，不一致

## Useful Grammar and Sentence Patterns

1. **“This position allows + noun + to + verb”**  
   Use this pattern to explain how a location or situation makes an action possible.  
   Example: “This position allows it to receive useful information about light.”

2. **“As + change happens, + result”**  
   Use this pattern when one event develops at the same time as another.  
   Example: “As evening arrives and the environment becomes darker, the brain begins to increase production of melatonin.”

3. **“The goal is to + verb”**  
   Use this pattern to state the main purpose of an action or plan.  
   Example: “The goal is to give your internal clock clear signals and enough time to complete the rest your brain and body require.”
