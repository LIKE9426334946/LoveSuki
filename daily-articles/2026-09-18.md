# Why Bread Becomes Stale

Fresh bread has a soft center, a pleasant smell, and sometimes a crisp crust. Leave it for a day or two, however, and the center becomes firm while the crust loses its crispness. People often say that stale bread has simply dried out. Moisture loss matters, but the main process is more interesting: the starch inside the bread slowly changes its structure.

Flour contains tiny starch grains. When dough is baked, heat and water cause these grains to swell and lose their original ordered shape. This process is called gelatinization. The changed starch helps create the soft structure of fresh bread and holds water within it.

After the bread cools, some starch molecules begin to arrange themselves into a more ordered form again. Scientists call this process retrogradation. As the molecules reorganize, they hold water differently and make the crumb—the soft inside part of bread—firmer. This is why bread can become stale even when it is kept in a closed bag and has not lost much water to the air.

Water also moves within the loaf. It may travel from the moist center toward the drier crust. As a result, the crumb feels drier and harder, while a once-crisp crust may become soft or rubbery. Later, if the bread is left uncovered, more water escapes into the air and the entire loaf becomes dry.

Temperature has a surprising effect on staling. A refrigerator keeps many foods fresh, but ordinary bread often becomes stale faster there. Cool temperatures above freezing encourage starch molecules to reorganize. If you plan to eat bread within a day or two, keeping it well wrapped at room temperature is usually better. For longer storage, freezing is more effective because very low temperatures greatly slow the movement of molecules.

When freezing bread, separate it into slices first and place it in a tightly closed bag. You can then remove only what you need. Toasting frozen slices directly is often convenient. If you freeze a whole loaf, wrapping it carefully helps prevent freezer burn and unwanted smells.

Heating stale bread can temporarily make it softer. Heat breaks some of the new connections formed between starch molecules and allows water to move again. An oven or toaster can therefore improve both the crumb and crust. However, the effect does not last forever. As the bread cools, it will begin to firm up again, often more quickly than before.

Staling is different from mold. Stale bread may be unpleasant but is not automatically unsafe. Moldy bread, by contrast, should be thrown away because growth can extend farther inside than the visible spots. Reheating does not reliably make moldy bread safe.

Understanding staling gives you a simple storage rule: wrap bread well, avoid the refrigerator for short-term storage, and use the freezer when you want to keep it longer.

## Important Vocabulary

1. **stale** — no longer fresh or pleasant to eat; 不新鲜的，变陈的
2. **crust** — the hard outer surface of bread; 面包皮
3. **moisture** — a small amount of water contained in something; 水分
4. **starch** — a substance found in foods such as flour, rice, and potatoes; 淀粉
5. **swell** — to become larger or rounder; 膨胀
6. **gelatinization** — the process in which starch changes when heated with water; 糊化
7. **crumb** — the soft inside part of bread; 面包瓤
8. **reorganize** — to arrange something into a new structure or order; 重新组织
9. **rubbery** — unpleasantly soft, tough, and difficult to bite; 像橡胶一样有韧性的
10. **mold** — a type of fungus that grows on old food or damp surfaces; 霉菌

## Useful Grammar and Sentence Patterns

1. **“This is why + clause”**  
   Use this pattern to connect an explanation with its result.  
   Example: “This is why bread can become stale even when it is kept in a closed bag.”

2. **“If you plan to + verb, …”**  
   Use this pattern to give advice based on someone's intention.  
   Example: “If you plan to eat bread within a day or two, keeping it well wrapped at room temperature is usually better.”

3. **“by contrast”**  
   Use this phrase to introduce a clear difference between two things.  
   Example: “Stale bread may be unpleasant but is not automatically unsafe. Moldy bread, by contrast, should be thrown away.”
