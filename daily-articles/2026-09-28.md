# Why We Get Goosebumps

A cold wind touches your arms, and suddenly your skin changes. Tiny bumps appear, and the hairs on your body stand more upright. The same thing may happen when you hear powerful music, feel afraid, or experience a strong memory. These small bumps are called goosebumps, and they are created by an automatic response in the nervous system.

Each hair on your skin grows from a small structure called a hair follicle. Attached to many follicles is a tiny muscle called the arrector pili muscle. You cannot control this muscle consciously. When the sympathetic nervous system becomes active, the muscle contracts and pulls the hair into a more upright position. The skin around the follicle is also pulled slightly, creating the raised bump that you can see.

Cold is one common trigger. In many furry animals, standing the hairs up traps a thicker layer of air close to the body. That trapped air acts as insulation and slows heat loss. Humans have much less body hair, so goosebumps do not keep us very warm. However, the response is probably a leftover from ancestors whose thicker hair made it more useful.

Fear can produce the same reaction for a different reason. The sympathetic nervous system is part of the body's rapid response to possible danger. It can increase heart rate, change blood flow, and prepare muscles for action. In animals with thick fur, raised hair can also make the body look larger, which may help frighten an enemy. A frightened cat with puffed-up fur is a familiar example.

Strong emotions can cause goosebumps even when there is no cold or danger. Music, a moving speech, or an important memory may activate brain systems connected with emotion and reward. These systems can interact with the autonomic nervous system, which controls automatic body functions. As a result, an emotional experience can create a physical change in the skin.

Scientists sometimes study this response to learn how emotion affects the body. Goosebumps are useful because they are visible, but they are not a perfect measure of feeling. A person can be deeply moved without developing goosebumps, and cold air can create them without any emotional meaning.

The word "goosebumps" comes from the appearance of the skin after the feathers have been removed from a goose. The small raised points look similar. Other languages use different animal comparisons or simply describe the skin as rough or raised.

Goosebumps usually disappear when the trigger ends. The tiny muscles relax, the hairs return to their normal angle, and the skin becomes smooth again. The whole process can happen without any conscious decision.

So when your favorite song sends a wave of bumps across your arms, your body is using an ancient automatic system. The reaction may no longer help a human grow a thick protective coat, but it still reveals how closely the brain, nerves, muscles, and skin work together.

## Important Vocabulary

1. **goosebumps** — small raised areas on the skin that appear when you are cold or emotional; 鸡皮疙瘩
2. **follicle** — a small structure in the skin from which a hair grows; 毛囊
3. **contract** — to become shorter or tighter; 收缩
4. **sympathetic nervous system** — the part of the nervous system that prepares the body for action or stress; 交感神经系统
5. **insulation** — material or a layer that slows the movement of heat; 隔热，保温
6. **ancestor** — a person or animal from which later generations developed; 祖先
7. **frighten** — to make someone or something feel afraid; 使害怕
8. **autonomic** — relating to body processes that happen automatically; 自主的
9. **trigger** — something that causes a reaction or event to begin; 诱因，触发因素
10. **visible** — able to be seen; 可见的

## Useful Grammar and Sentence Patterns

1. **“When + clause, + result”**  
   Use this pattern to explain what happens after a condition or event.  
   Example: “When the sympathetic nervous system becomes active, the muscle contracts.”

2. **“so + adjective/adverb + that …”**  
   Use this pattern to show that something reaches a degree that causes a result.  
   Example: “Humans have much less body hair, so goosebumps do not keep us very warm.”

3. **“even when + clause”**  
   Use this pattern to show that something is true in a surprising or unusual situation.  
   Example: “Strong emotions can cause goosebumps even when there is no cold or danger.”
