# Why Your Recorded Voice Sounds Strange

Most people are surprised the first time they hear a clear recording of their own voice. It may sound higher, thinner, or simply unfamiliar. Some people even dislike it and wonder, “Do I really sound like that?” The recording is usually accurate. The strange feeling comes from the fact that you hear your own voice in two different ways when you speak.

The first way is through air conduction. Sound waves leave your mouth, travel through the air, and enter your ears. They make your eardrums vibrate, just as another person’s voice does. A microphone mainly records this part of your voice. When you play the recording, the sound again travels through the air to your ears.

However, while you are speaking, you also hear your voice through bone conduction. Vibrations from your vocal cords move through the bones and tissues of your head directly toward the inner ear. This path strengthens some lower frequencies. As a result, the voice you normally hear inside your head often seems deeper and fuller than the voice other people hear.

A recording removes most of this bone-conducted sound. You hear only the air-conducted version, so the balance of frequencies is different from what you expect. The equipment can make an additional difference. Phone microphones, room echoes, speakers, and audio compression may change the sound slightly. Still, even a high-quality recording can seem strange because it does not include your usual internal vibrations.

Psychology also plays a role. You have heard your voice from inside your head for thousands of hours, so your brain has built a strong idea of how it should sound. A recording conflicts with that familiar model. In addition, people often pay more attention to their own pauses, pronunciation, and tone than they would when listening to someone else. This extra self-awareness can make small details feel more important than they really are.

The unfamiliarity usually becomes weaker with repeated listening. Singers, actors, teachers, and video creators often learn to judge their recorded voices more calmly because they hear them regularly. They may also improve their speaking by noticing speed, volume, unclear words, or repeated sounds such as “um.”

If you want to become more comfortable with your recorded voice, begin with short clips. Record thirty seconds of speech, listen for one useful detail, and avoid judging your whole personality from the sound. Try different distances from the microphone and record in a quiet room. Over time, the recorded version becomes another familiar form of your voice.

Your voice has not suddenly changed when you press the play button. You are simply hearing it through a path that other people use every day but that your own brain experiences less often.

## Important Vocabulary

1. **unfamiliar** — not known or recognized; 陌生的，不熟悉的
2. **accurate** — correct and close to the truth; 准确的
3. **conduction** — the movement of heat, sound, or electricity through a material; 传导
4. **eardrum** — a thin part inside the ear that vibrates when sound reaches it; 鼓膜
5. **vibrate** — to move quickly backward and forward; 振动
6. **vocal cords** — two bands of tissue in the throat that produce the voice; 声带
7. **frequency** — the rate at which a sound wave repeats; 频率
8. **compression** — a process that reduces or controls the size or range of something; 压缩
9. **conflict** — to be different from or disagree with something; 冲突，不一致
10. **self-awareness** — conscious knowledge of your own behavior or feelings; 自我意识

## Useful Grammar and Sentence Patterns

1. **“The first time + clause”**  
   Use this pattern to describe what happens during someone’s first experience.  
   Example: “Most people are surprised the first time they hear a clear recording of their own voice.”

2. **“As a result, + consequence”**  
   Use this pattern to introduce the effect of the previous information.  
   Example: “As a result, the voice you normally hear inside your head often seems deeper and fuller.”

3. **“become more comfortable with + noun”**  
   Use this pattern to describe gradually feeling more relaxed about something.  
   Example: “If you want to become more comfortable with your recorded voice, begin with short clips.”
