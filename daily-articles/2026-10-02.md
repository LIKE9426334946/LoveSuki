# Why Leaves Change Color in Autumn

In many places, green summer leaves turn yellow, orange, red, or purple before they fall. The change can make an entire forest look different within a few weeks. It may seem that cold weather paints new colors onto the leaves. In fact, many of those colors were already present, while others are produced as the tree prepares for winter.

Leaves look green because they contain a large amount of chlorophyll. This pigment absorbs energy from sunlight and helps the plant make sugar through photosynthesis. Chlorophyll does not last forever, so a healthy tree continually produces more of it during the growing season. The strong green color usually hides other pigments in the leaf.

As autumn approaches, days become shorter and temperatures often fall. Trees respond to these changes by slowing chlorophyll production. They also begin moving useful nutrients, especially nitrogen, out of the leaves and storing them in branches, trunks, and roots. As the remaining chlorophyll breaks down, the green color fades.

Yellow and orange pigments called carotenoids then become easier to see. Carotenoids are present in many leaves throughout spring and summer, where they help collect light and protect the leaf from too much energy. They are also responsible for colors in foods such as carrots and corn. Because they break down more slowly than chlorophyll, their colors remain visible after the green disappears.

Red and purple colors have a somewhat different story. They often come from pigments called anthocyanins. Some leaves produce more anthocyanins during autumn as sugars become trapped inside them. Bright sunlight and cool nights can encourage this process. Scientists think these pigments may protect leaf cells from strong light while the tree recovers valuable nutrients. They may also discourage some insects, although their exact benefits can vary among species.

Weather affects the strength and timing of autumn color. Warm, sunny days followed by cool nights often produce bright reds. A severe frost can damage leaves and end the display early. Drought may cause leaves to turn brown or fall before their colors fully develop. Rain, wind, tree health, and local temperature all influence what people see in a particular year.

While the colors are changing, the tree is also preparing to release each leaf. A special layer of cells forms where the leaf stem joins the branch. This layer gradually blocks the movement of water and nutrients. Eventually, the connection becomes weak enough for wind or gravity to remove the leaf. The tree then seals the small opening, reducing water loss and protecting itself from infection.

Losing leaves helps many trees survive winter. Frozen soil can make water difficult to absorb, while broad leaves continue to lose moisture. Snow and ice can also add damaging weight. By dropping their leaves and entering a quieter state, deciduous trees save water and energy until growing conditions improve.

Evergreen trees follow a different strategy. Their needles have tough surfaces and shapes that limit water loss, so they can keep most of them through winter. Even evergreens replace old needles, but they usually do so gradually rather than all at once.

Autumn color is therefore not simply decoration. It is a visible sign of pigments changing, nutrients moving, and trees carefully preparing for a difficult season.

## Important Vocabulary

1. **pigment** — a substance that gives color to a material or living tissue; 色素
2. **chlorophyll** — the green pigment that helps plants absorb light for photosynthesis; 叶绿素
3. **photosynthesis** — the process plants use to make food from light, water, and carbon dioxide; 光合作用
4. **nutrient** — a substance that a living thing needs to grow and stay healthy; 营养物质
5. **carotenoid** — a yellow, orange, or red pigment found in plants; 类胡萝卜素
6. **anthocyanin** — a pigment that can give plants red, purple, or blue colors; 花青素
7. **drought** — a long period with little or no rain; 干旱
8. **release** — to let something go or allow it to separate; 释放，使脱落
9. **deciduous** — describing a tree that loses its leaves during part of the year; 落叶的
10. **evergreen** — a tree or plant that keeps green leaves throughout the year; 常绿植物

## Useful Grammar and Sentence Patterns

1. **“As + event develops, + change happens”**  
   Use this pattern to show that one change happens while another process develops.  
   Example: “As the remaining chlorophyll breaks down, the green color fades.”

2. **“be responsible for + noun”**  
   Use this pattern to explain what causes a particular result.  
   Example: “Carotenoids are also responsible for colors in foods such as carrots and corn.”

3. **“By + verb-ing, + subject + verb”**  
   Use this pattern to explain how an action produces a result.  
   Example: “By dropping their leaves and entering a quieter state, deciduous trees save water and energy.”
